What it does

Six small things, each built to remove a reason to stop: looking up a word, keeping it, and meeting it again.

⌕

Look up any word

Search a word and get its senses grouped by part of speech, each with a definition and a real example sentence — not a wall of every meaning at once. New lookups are written on the fly, so they ask you to sign in first.

✎

Save it in a tap

Keep the word from the same screen you looked it up on. Mark anything you fumbled for later, and it moves to the front of the queue next time.

◈

Guess the word

You see the definition, you produce the word. Recall in the direction you'll actually need it — reaching for a word you mean rather than recognising one you're shown.

▤

Rebuild the sentence

Write the word into a sentence of your own and get it read back with what worked and what didn't. Knowing a definition and using it are different skills.

◔

A nudge at 20:00

One notification a day with five words waiting. The words stay out of the message — a lock screen is no place for vocabulary — and tapping it opens the session already loaded.

⤓

Installs like an app

Add it to your home screen and it opens like anything else on the phone — full screen, its own icon, no app store in the way.

Why I built it

The words a textbook leaves out.

I've always been pulled in by how people communicate. English isn't my first language, so I got to learn it twice — once from textbooks, and once from listening to how people actually talk.

The second lesson never really ends. My Irish colleagues don't try to do something; they take a stab at it. Same meaning, but only one of them sounds like a person. Those are the words I started keeping — the rare ones, the phrases that don't survive a word-for-word translation, the ones that let you say what you mean exactly instead of approximately.

They work in the other direction too. Recognising the phrase someone just chose is often the difference between following a sentence and understanding what was meant by it — what got implied, softened, or left between the lines.

So I built the thing I wanted for collecting them, with the practice that works on me: guess the word from its definition, or write it into a sentence and see what comes back.

How it works

01

Find a word

Look it up, read the sense you meant, and save it. It lands in your list with the definition attached.

02

Practice it

Pick a mode and a handful of words. Two minutes of guessing and writing does more than a re-read ever did.

03

Let it return

The evening reminder brings five words back — the ones you marked first, topped up from the rest of your list.
